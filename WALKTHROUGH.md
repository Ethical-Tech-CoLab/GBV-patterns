# Walkthrough: presenting GBV Patterns

Site: https://ethical-tech-colab.github.io/GBV-patterns/
Time: about 12 minutes at a walking pace, 7 if you skip the By the Numbers and What if pages.

## Before you start

- Open the site in a private window so no stale copy of the scripts is cached, and hard-refresh once (Cmd+Shift+R).
- Press **P** on any page for presenter mode: the header and footer disappear, each section fills the screen in large type, the arrow keys step section by section, Esc exits. You can also open any page with `?present=1` on the end of the address.
- Leave the theme dark. The map patterns have not been checked on the light theme.
- Have the Home page loaded before you speak. The hero silhouettes and the map need a second to draw.

## 1. Home (2 to 3 minutes)

**Hero.** Silhouettes of women and men drift across the globe. Say: the figures are the same isotype used across the site; no faces, no photographs of victims anywhere on the platform.

**What is femicide.** The UN definition, then the six forms named by the NYC Coalition to End Femicide and Sanctuary for Families. Point at the fourth: sex trade-related femicide. That is the one this site is about.

**Why the sex trade.** Read the second paragraph aloud: no government or agency in the U.S. or New York counts the women and girls killed in the sex trade by sex buyers, pimps and traffickers. Everything that follows hangs on that sentence.

**Every ten minutes.** The dots light up at the 2024 global pace, from the moment the page opened. Glance at the count and say how many since you began speaking.

**Of every ten women.** Click two chips: 9 of 10 killed by a man they knew, then 0 of 10 in the sex trade appear in any official count. The same ten figures each time so the eye compares.

**The first step for New York.** The ask, in CATW's words: name and codify femicide.

**The map.** This is the centrepiece. Three moves:
1. Say the headline: 30 countries address femicide in law, 23 name it as a crime, the United States is not among them.
2. Click **France**. Read the observation: no offence called femicide, but stricter homicide penalties for a gender-motivated killing. Then click the **United States**: nothing found in the three sources. That contrast is the point.
3. Click the legend entry **Femicide is a crime (23)** to drop the list, with Costa Rica 2007 at the top and Italy 2025 at the bottom. Click it again to close.

Skip the year chips unless asked. Do not use pinch-zoom on a projector; the + button works.

## 2. Femicide in the Sex Trade (1 minute)

The four short sections: why these deaths are femicide, how women die, why no one counts, how the deaths are reported.

Then **What the health record says**: women in prostitution murdered at nearly eighteen times the rate of women of the same age and race (Potterat 2004), strangulation as the signature (Glass 2008), New York's strangulation arrests, the medical cost per assault. Say once that every number on the page links to its primary source.

## 3. New York (2 minutes)

**Headline.** New York State must be first in the U.S. to define femicide in law. Four tiles: 0 states, 23 countries (the tile links back to the map), ? women killed in the sex trade in New York, 2021 the year the Sex Trade Survivors Justice and Equality Act was introduced.

**Real cases.** Flip one card, Gilgo Beach, and read the back: how the system named the crime versus what it was.

**In memoriam.** Seven women from Sex Industry Kills. Do not linger; the point is that a German volunteer memorial holds New York deaths that no New York agency records.

**The buyers.** The map of 141 reviews across ten regions from CATW's 2022 report, with the three 2026 cases as orange markers at the region. Say what it is not: no review text, no addresses, no usernames; only counts by region.

**The bill, session by session.** Three sessions, three referrals to the Codes committee, no vote.

## 4. By the Numbers (optional, 1 minute)

Open only if the audience is research or data people. The uncounted line at the top, then the two databases year by year and the questions the project puts to the data. Say plainly that the aggregated charts are off until Dawn Wilcox and CATW agree terms for Women Count USA.

## 5. What if…? (optional, 1 minute)

The scenarios: what New York would see if it looked. The Swiss cheese interactive here is the one place the "holes in the system" model still appears as a picture.

## 6. About CATW (30 seconds)

Who CATW is, what it does, the legacy timeline 1988 to 2026, and the two asks of New York. Hand to Taina or Nora if they are in the room.

## 7. Connecting the Dots (3 minutes, the close)

**Learn their stories.** Figures walk toward you from the light. Three kinds, with a key under the heading. Dark red: click for her story. Grey: Jane Doe, an estimate. Black shadows: a sex-buyer region and what the arrest record shows there.
- Click a dark red figure. You will get a woman from the memorial or the public record: Shao Ling Huang, killed in a Flushing massage parlor two weeks ago; one of the Gilgo Beach women; one of the ten women Arthur Shawcross killed in Rochester. Each card links its sources.
- Click a grey figure. Read the Jane Doe card aloud: her figure is an estimate; no one counts, so we might never actually know who she was, or how many there are.
- Press Esc to close the card. Click one more, then stop. Three is enough.

**Observations.** The ten variables from the first version of the game, ladders and chutes. Mention them as the frame CATW gave the project; do not read the list.

**The Gilgo Beach drive.** Press Start. On the left, the advocacy and the laws against online sexual exploitation; on the right, the murders and the investigation. Drive to 2013 and stop at Operation Flush the Johns and the backlash that followed, then let it run to the April 2026 plea. The red barrier is the eleven-year hiatus. Close on the heading: this is a case study in how to prevent femicide in the sex trade.

End there. Do not go back to Home.

## Questions you will get, and the answer on the site

- "Where do the numbers come from?" Every figure has a source line beneath it and an entry on the Sources page with a Verify link.
- "Why 30 countries when the World Bank says 29?" The World Bank does not publish its list. The 30 are the ones this site could name from ECLAC, UN Women and the World Bank blog; the caption under the map says so.
- "Did you scrape the buyer forums?" No. Index-level counts only, from CATW's published report; the method page explains the protocol and what is never collected. The shadows carry the arrest record by region, not forum text.
- "Why doesn't the FBI count this?" By the Numbers has the answer in four boxes: the UN framework's eight indicators, the FBI's voluntary and thinning data, the CDC's system without a femicide field, and New York's missing definition.
- "Are the silhouettes real women?" The figures are drawings. The stories behind the dark red ones are real and sourced; the grey ones stand for women no one counted.

## What is missing, as a presenter would feel it

**Things the audience will look for and not find**
1. **A New York number.** The headline tile says "?". The site argues that the absence is the finding, but a room wants one sourced count, even a floor: the seven women in the memorial, the eight Gilgo Beach women, the ten in Rochester, Shao Ling Huang. A single line "at least 26 women we can name, from three cases and one memorial" would carry the argument. Needs CATW's agreement on wording.
2. **The five steps.** The New York page lost its placeholder grid today. What New York can do now lives only on About CATW as two asks. The audience will ask "so what do you want Albany to do" and the answer has to be on the New York page in CATW's words.
3. **The memorial photographs.** The seven memorial cards reproduce photographs from Sex Industry Kills with attribution; the line under them says permission is still to be confirmed with the project. Confirm it or remove the images before the talk.
4. **The county view.** Only New York City has regional numbers. The DCJS request is on the backlog.
5. **Sound and motion on a projector.** The hero, the pace dots and the corridor all animate. On a weak projector the grey Jane Doe figures may vanish into the background. Test on the room's screen; the figure colours are two lines of CSS in game.html.

**Things that are built but not visible enough**
6. Done: Sources is in the main nav.
7. **The Invisible Men method page** (how buyer quotes would be collected under a protocol) is reachable only from a footer line on Connecting the Dots. If Elly is in the room, it deserves a mention.
8. Done: the footer says "Press P on any page to present".

**Things that could not be verified, and have to be said as such**
9. Operation Flush the Johns: the lawsuit is now verified (Louis DiMaria, News 12, March 2014). The age range 17 to 79 is not on any readable page and is not on the site.
10. The Rochester line "10 of 12 women were in street prostitution on Lyell Avenue": UPI and AP in January 1990 say at least nine of the eleven were prostitutes, which is now on the cards. No readable source names Lyell Avenue.
11. The Gilgo plea and sentence are now verified from PBS/AP, CBS and FOX 5: plea 8 April 2026, life without parole on 17 June 2026. The sentence structure CATW's timeline gave (consecutive terms) differs between outlets and is not used.
12. Shao Ling Huang's 17 November court date: no report found. The card says the case is open.
13. The 40 to 42 million and 75% figures are verified in the Fondation Scelles document (page 5, attributed to the UN, 2009). The 80% women share is not in it and rests on a 2017 PRI article; the home page says so.

**Things a decision-maker will want next**
14. A cost. The health section has the medical cost of one assault; nothing yet says what one femicide costs a county. The WISQARS run is on the backlog.
15. A comparison. Sweden's two points (1996, 2014) stand alone. Norway, France and Ireland would make the demand argument a trend, but no primary series has been found.
16. Done: Connecting the Dots now ends on a card with the two asks and S2005/A1465.
