Economic impact

If lives do not move a legislature, money might. France was persuaded by a cost study. This page summarises it, then counts what New York loses.

The French study: ProstCost

In 2015 the Mouvement du Nid and the social-science firm Psytel spent eighteen months costing the system of prostitution in France. They identified 29 cost items in six categories, published every calculation online, and called the result a floor. A year later France passed its Equality Model law.

€1.6 billiona year. What prostitution costs French society, by the study's conservative estimate. The money "made" by the sex trade is counted as a cost: most of it never reaches the tax base.

Where the money goes

Buyers pay €3.2 billion a year. At least 45% leaves the country through pimping networks. If buyers spent that money on anything else, the state would recover at least €853 million in tax.

In memoriam, 2014

France keeps no official statistic on the homicide of people in prostitution. From its own delegations and the press, the Mouvement du Nid counted seven murders in 2014. The study priced the deaths at €189 million to €267 million. These are the ones it listed.

31 December 2014, ÉpinalA 45-year-old woman from Brazil, stabbed 53 times by a 33-year-old buyer who told police he was "not satisfied with the service".
14 November 2014, Paris, Porte de VincennesA young woman killed with a blade. The killer is unknown.
5 September 2014, LunelAn Albanian woman in her thirties found dead, naked, in a canal near Montpellier. The killer is unknown.
14 June 2014, Clichy-la-GarenneLuz Maria, 32, from the Dominican Republic, stabbed repeatedly in a hotel. The suspect is a "regular client", 38.
19 May 2014, ChâteaurouxA woman of 64, killed by blows to the head. The killer is unknown.
1 April 2014, ParisA Chinese woman in her fifties, stabbed about ten times in a flat on rue Bichat. The suspect is a buyer.
March 2014, HaguenauA woman of 51, stabbed at home by a "regular client". His DNA tied him to the strangling of a 59-year-old woman in Strasbourg in July 2013.

Mouvement du Nid and Psytel, ProstCost: Estimation du coût économique et social de la prostitution en France, synthesis, May 2015. Funded 90% by the European Commission's ISEC programme. Every cost sheet is public on the study's site. Mortality was estimated from Potterat et al., American Journal of Epidemiology, 2004. The authors state the estimate is a minimum: costs to families, to buyers' households and to property values were left out for lack of data.

What other countries have put a number on

Where governments and development banks have costed violence against women, the figures land between a third of a percent and a tenth of GDP, depending on what is counted. None of these studies is directly comparable with another, and each says its estimate is conservative.

0.31% of GDP
cost of killings of women in Latin America, more than double the global average of 0.12%. Inter-American Development Bank, October 2026.
5.12% / 4.3%
of GDP: cost of violence against women in Paraguay and Ecuador, combined-methodology estimates cited by the IDB.
€4,933 million
Spain, 0.37% of GDP: health, labour-productive and legal-police costs borne by women, families, the state, business and civil society.
0.8% to 2.1%
of GDP in Argentina, bottom-up accounting of domestic violence in the last 12 months only. Excludes femicide, under-18s, over-65s and violence outside the home.
Sh41 billion
lost by Kenya every year to gender-based violence including femicide, with GDP losses put at 7.8% to 10%. Kenya's 2025 presidential taskforce, from Kenyatta University's Women's Economic Empowerment Hub.
725
femicides recorded in Kenya in 2024, 2.66 per 100,000 women, about 47 a week, three quarters by someone the victim knew. Cited from UNODC by the Daily Nation.
1.2% to 3.7%
of GDP lost to violence against women in developing countries, the global range cited by the Kenyan taskforce. South Africa's own estimate is 0.9% to 1.3%.
Sh109,551
average cost of care per survivor and family in Nakuru County, Kenya: medical bills, property damage, lost income, missed work and school days. The kind of per-case figure New York does not yet publish.

Kenya's earlier 2016 estimate: Sh46 billion a year, of which Sh10 billion out-of-pocket medical costs and Sh33 billion lost productivity. National Gender and Equality Commission.
