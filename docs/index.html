The gap this project exists to close

Roughly three dozen countries define femicide in their criminal codes. The United States and New York State do not. No agency counts the women killed in the sex trade. When those deaths are reported at all, the language used often hides what happened.

~36

Countries that name femicide in law

Most are in Latin America and Europe. Naming the crime is what makes it countable, and counting is what makes it a policy problem rather than a series of private tragedies.

Official counts of women killed in the sex trade in New York

Not zero deaths. Zero ledgers. The only systematic record in the country is a volunteer database, Women Count USA, built from news reports, social media and obituaries.

How a femicide disappears from the record

Each step below is a place where a death can be reclassified, softened or dropped. Open one to see what the record says and what it could say instead.

The relationship is marked unknown40,142 reports in 2024
One in six NYC domestic incident reports in 2024 had no recorded relationship between the parties. A buyer, a pimp or a trafficker is not a partner or a relative, so these cases fall outside both categories the city tracks.
The headline says "sex worker", not "woman"Media framing
Describing a victim by the circumstances of her exploitation shifts the reader's attention from the person who killed her to the life she was living. CATW's review of coverage looks for this pattern case by case.
The death is filed as an overdose or an accidentCause of death
Deaths in the sex trade often involve drugs, exposure or injury. When a medical examiner records only the proximate cause, the coercion behind it is never entered anywhere.
The perpetrator's relationship is never recordedData model
Age, race and relationship to the victim are what make patterns visible across cases. CATW's priority for this project is to capture those fields for perpetrators, not names.

The count of countries recognising femicide is an approximate figure from CATW's project brief. Women Count USA is maintained by Dawn Wilcox at womencountusa.org.

Why a page, not a PDF

Technology can reach new audiences. For New York policymakers it can reach the same audience in a different way: a number they can press, a borough they can pick, a gap they can see.

Open data, updated

Every figure here traces to a public source. The database and framework behind it will be open source and refreshed as new reports land.

Patterns, not profiles

The project works from aggregated demographics and relationship data. No victim is identified. No individual is tracked.
