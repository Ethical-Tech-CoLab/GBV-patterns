Femicide in New York State

Around the world, femicide is an epidemic, including in the sex trade. New York is poised to become the first US state to codify femicide into law.

×
This site documents violence against women and girls, including killing. Support in New York: the 24-hour HOPE hotline, 1-800-621-4673. National Domestic Violence Hotline, 1-800-799-7233.

The background

Twenty-three countries name femicide as a crime. In 2024 New York City counted 66 domestic violence homicides, 33 of them by an intimate partner. The state counted 63 intimate partner homicides in 2022. The women killed in the sex trade are in none of these counts.

The problem

A state cannot count what it does not name. A woman killed by a buyer, a pimp or a trafficker is a homicide with "relationship unknown". No agency records her as what she was, so no pattern can be seen and no budget line can be argued. The buyers are rarely arrested: 118 patronizing arrests in all of New York in 2023.

The solution

Name femicide in the Penal Law and include the sex trade. Attach a reporting duty, so every agency counts the same thing. Enforce the patronizing laws already on the books. Pass the Sex Trade Survivors Justice and Equality Act, S2005 and A1465, referred to Codes in January 2026. Count the cost, the way France did. The five steps.

66 and 33: NYC ENDGBV 2024 fact sheet. 63: DCJS 2022 Domestic Homicide Report. 118: New York State Unified Court System. S2005 and A1465: New York State Senate. All on the sources page.

What is femicide?

The United Nations defines femicide as an intentional killing with a gender-related motivation, and the most extreme and brutal manifestation of violence against women and girls.

This project is inspired by the New York City Coalition to End Femicide, initiated by Sanctuary for Families, New York's leading service provider and advocate for survivors of domestic violence, sex trafficking and related forms of gender violence. The initiative includes different forms of femicide:

Intimate partner femicide

Current or former partners.

Non-partner femicide

Family members, acquaintances, or strangers.

Reproductive-related femicide

Pregnancy, postpartum status, or reproductive control.

Sex trade-related femicide

Prostitution and commercial sexual exploitation.

Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women

Colonial violence and systemic neglect.

Transfemicide

Misogyny, transphobia, and racism.

In the United States, femicide cases are treated as isolated incidents, not as part of a larger pattern and evidence of a culture's inherent misogyny and normalization of violence against women and girls.

The first step for New York is to name and codify femicide

Homicide is a crime under the law. However, given the reported high statistics on gender-based murders, it is important for every jurisdiction to collect data, identify, and prosecute crimes men commit against women and girls that are specifically gender-based.

Collecting data will help illuminate femicide as a systemic failure, rather than unrelated, private tragedies, and an issue that urgently deserves legislative action.

Twenty-three countries define femicide in their criminal codes. The United States and New York State do not.

Counting without a law

There are also a number of countries that collect data on the number of femicides even in the absence of laws that define it, such as Sweden. To address the lack of comparable data on violence against women, EIGE developed 13 indicators with uniform definitions of the multiple forms of intimate partner violence, femicide and rape, with a published methodology and metadata per country.
