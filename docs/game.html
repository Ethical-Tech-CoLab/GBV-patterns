      Case study in how to prevent femicide in the sex trade: the Gilgo Beach murders case

      In 2010, a search for Shannan Gilbert, a missing woman who had been in the online sex trade, led to the discovery of ten other bodies on a stretch of Long Island's South Shore called Gilgo Beach. In a case that spans three decades, Rex Heuermann admitted to killing eight women. There are likely many more.

      The timeline holds two stories at once: the Gilgo Beach murders, and the advocacy, laws and other efforts to combat online sexual exploitation that happened throughout. Read together, they show how change that counteracts impunity can happen, and how our system can prevent femicide.

        What New York can do

        1. Name and codify femicide in state law, so that every killing of a woman because she is female is counted as one.
        2. Pass the Sex Trade Survivors Justice and Equality Act: decriminalize the women, hold buyers, pimps and traffickers accountable, and fund exit services.
