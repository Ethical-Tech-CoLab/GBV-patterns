New York › Sex buyers

The buyers are visible. The arrests are not.

In 2022 CATW read hundreds of posts on "hobby boards" where sex buyers across New York State review the women they bought, and placed each one on a map. The demand side of the sex trade is not hidden; it writes reviews. This page rebuilds that map as an open layer and sets it against the arrest record, which is the measure of impunity.

Content warning: the original reviews are violent, racist and degrading. They are not reproduced here. Each point marks only where a review was posted from. The full report, with the quotes, is linked below.

Time served: sex crimes against white-collar crimes

The comparison people expect is that rapists get off lightly against tax evaders. The federal numbers say the opposite, and that is the point: when a sexual abuser is sentenced, the sentence is long. He is almost never sentenced. Impunity is not in the courtroom; it is in the thousand cases that never reach one.

Federal cases are a small, severe slice of all sex crimes; most are prosecuted, or not, in state court.

The enforcement layer

Forum activity and arrests are two different things, so they sit in two different layers. This one is the arrest record for every prostitution-related charge in New York State, by who was arrested: the people sold, the buyers, the pimps and premises, the traffickers. Through 2019 the people sold were arrested far more often than the buyers. In 2023 the buyers were arrested 118 times statewide.

Who the state arrests

Two lines, fifteen years. The women sold, and the men who buy. In 2009 New York arrested more than two women for every buyer. In 2023, for the first time in this series, it arrested slightly more buyers than women: 118 against 104. Press play.

Sweden, before and after the buyer law

Share of Swedish men who report ever having paid for sex. Buying sex became a crime in 1999.

12.7% in 1996 (the national sex survey, as reported in later peer-reviewed studies) and 7.5% in 2014 (Stockholm county administrative board, Prostitution in Sweden 2014). The 2008 and 2011 survey values are reported differently by different sources and are not drawn. In a six-month window, 0.29% of Swedish men reported buying sex against 0.93% in Norway and 1.3% in Denmark, which had no buyer law at the time (Kotsadam and Jakobsson). Whether the law caused the fall is disputed; the sources page carries both readings. Before-and-after series for Norway, France and Ireland were not found in a primary source and are not shown.
