What CATW does

The Coalition Against Trafficking in Women has worked since 1988 to end the trafficking and sexual exploitation of women and girls. Its position is that prostitution and sex trafficking are gender-based violence, and that the law should target the demand, not the women. This site is the data arm of that argument for New York.

Advocating for strong laws

Laws that provide access to justice, target demand and ensure women and girls are free from the violence of the sex trade. CATW calls this the Equality Model. Read more

Raising public awareness

Presenting trafficking and sexual exploitation as sex-based and gender-based violence, and mobilising the public to press policymakers. Read more

Supporting survivor leadership

Partnering with survivors on prevention and protection policy, so the people with the most knowledge lead. Read more

What CATW is asking New York to do

Name femicide in state law, and include women killed in the sex tradeThis project
About three dozen countries define femicide in their criminal codes. No US state does. CATW's brief proposes that New York codify sex trafficking and prostitution as forms of femicide, so the deaths are counted and the pattern becomes a policy problem.
Close the gap survivors call the Epstein loopholeCivil justice
Epstein survivors have told New York legislators that his estate argues the statute of limitations makes their claims worth nothing, and that current law bars punitive damages against a deceased abuser's estate. CATW works with survivors pressing to close this.
Pass the Sex Trade Survivors Justice and Equality ActIntroduced 2021
The Equality Model bill for New York, developed with survivors and introduced in 2021: decriminalise the people sold, hold buyers and exploiters accountable, fund exit services. Maine passed the first state version in June 2023. New Yorkers for the Equality Model, with CATW, has carried it since 2019.
Finish what the 2018 law startedS5988A, S8723
In 2018 New York created the crime of sex trafficking of a child, removing the need to prove force, fraud or coercion for anyone under 18. Senator Myrie's S8723 would extend that to adults who are mentally disabled or incapacitated. The force, fraud or coercion test still applies to every other adult.
Make buying sex from a minor a felony everywherePassed
New York's Victims Protection and Child Sex Buyer Accountability Act made buying sex from 15 to 17 year olds a felony rather than a misdemeanour, closing a gap with federal law and other states.
Count the cost, the way France didEconomics
CATW points to a French report on the cost of prostitution to society that persuaded lawmakers there. The economics page on this site starts that accounting for New York.

Legacy of impact, 1988 to 2026

Our partner's own record, from its founding to the law New York is being asked to pass. Red marks the New York entries.

1988

CATW is founded

The First USA Conference on International Trafficking in Women launches the Coalition Against Trafficking in Women.

1995

Beijing: Violence Against Women Caucus

CATW chairs the caucus at the Fourth World Conference on Women.

1999

Sweden bans the purchase of sex

The first law to decriminalise prostituted women while holding buyers accountable. CATW supported the Swedish partners.

2000

UN Trafficking in Persons Protocol

The Palermo Protocol adopts a comprehensive definition of trafficking, after advocacy by the network CATW organised.

2001

First US sex trafficking report

Interviews with 40 women trafficked and prostituted in the United States.

2007

New York State Human Trafficking Act

Passes after a joint campaign.

2008 to 2009

Iceland and Norway adopt the model

The Swedish model becomes the Nordic Model.

2010

Craigslist closes Adult Services

After CATW's Global Campaign for a Sex Trafficking Free Internet.

2015

Federal and New York victims' laws

The Justice for Victims of Trafficking Act and New York's Trafficking Victims Protection and Justice Act are signed.

2016

France: the strongest law of its kind

The French Act Against Prostitution, after proceedings CATW Europe began in the early 2000s.

2017

Ireland: the model becomes the Equality Model

Criminal Law (Sexual Offences) Act 2015 enters force.

2018

FOSTA-SESTA

Websites knowingly facilitating sex trafficking can be held accountable.

2019

New Yorkers for the Equality Model

A survivor-led alliance of more than 30 advocates and organisations, CATW among them, begins work to bring the Equality Model to New York State.

2021

Sex Trade Survivors Justice and Equality Act

Developed with survivors and advocates, introduced to the New York State Legislature. Still not passed.

2023

Maine passes Equality Model legislation

The first US state to adopt the framework, in June 2023.

2024

UN Special Rapporteur's report on prostitution

Reem Alsalem calls on governments to understand the system of prostitution as exploitation and a human rights abuse. The Special Rapporteur Carolina and Yorke met at Apne Aap in October 2026.

2026

France marks ten years

Survivors and advocates gather in Paris for a decade of the abolitionist law.

Source: CATW, Legacy of Impact, about page, read 22 October 2026. Selected entries; the full timeline has 36.
