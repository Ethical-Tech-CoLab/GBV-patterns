Femicide in the sex trade

Currently, no agency in the U.S. or in New York counts the number of women and girls killed in the sex trade by sex buyers, pimps and traffickers.

The sex trade is an umbrella term for all forms of prostitution including escort services, illegal massage parlors, pornography, strip clubs, sugar dating, and street prostitution.

Why are sex trafficking and prostitution forms of femicide

Out of an estimated 40 to 42 million people in prostitution worldwide, roughly 80%, about 4 out of 5, are women and girls. Approximately 75% of individuals in the global sex trade are between the ages of 13 and 25.

CATW text: the killing of women in the sex trade as gender-based killing; the legal definitions that include it abroad.

How women die in the sex trade

CATW text and sources: homicide by buyers and exploiters; overdose, exposure, untreated injury and illness; the long-term toll.

Risk of being murdered: women in prostitution against all women

Standardised mortality ratio for homicide. Women of the same age and race in the general population set to 1. Colorado Springs cohort, 1,969 women, 1967 to 1999, the only long-term cohort study in the United States.
17.7×
the homicide rate of other women of the same age and race, while a woman is active in prostitution: 229 homicides per 100,000 woman-years. Potterat et al., 2004.
7.5×
the odds of a woman later being killed by her partner if he has strangled her before and she survived. Attempted murder: 6.7×. Glass et al., 2008. The emergency room sees her first.

strangulation arrests in New York State in 2023 against 2019, on the state's gender-based violence dashboard.

Potterat et al., Mortality in a long-term open cohort of prostitute women, American Journal of Epidemiology 2004: homicide SMR 17.7 (95% CI 6.2 to 29.3) during active prostitution, 229 per 100,000 person-years; all-cause SMR 5.9 while active, 1.9 over the whole cohort. Glass et al., Non-fatal strangulation is an important risk factor for homicide of women, Journal of Emergency Medicine 2008: adjusted odds ratio 7.48 (95% CI 4.53 to 12.35) for completed homicide, 6.70 (3.91 to 11.49) for attempted; a case-control study, so an association, not a causal multiplier.
