Five hypotheses about the backlash

New York City's domestic violence homicides moved from 62 in 2021 to 71 in 2022 to 73 in 2023 to 66 in 2024. Nearly one in four governments told UN Women they faced a backlash against women's rights in 2024. Each hypothesis below says what would be true if it held, and how this project will test it. None is confirmed yet.

H1We are not seeing more violence, we are counting it betterTestable now

If true

Reported incidents rise while deaths, which are hard to hide, stay flat. New York City's incident reports fell slightly from 2023 to 2024 while intimate partner homicides rose from 30 to 33, which cuts against a pure reporting effect.

How we test it

Compare CDC WISQARS and FBI female homicide series, which do not depend on willingness to report, with police incident reports and hotline calls, which do.

H2Social media and the manosphere are shifting attitudes among young menNeeds data

If true

Perpetrator age skews younger over time and misogynistic-attitude keywords appear more often in recent cases. Women Count USA codes incel traits, misogynistic hate speech and online grooming per case.

How we test it

Perpetrator age distribution by year from the database, plus attitude surveys such as the Global Institute for Women's Leadership and Ipsos tracking, once aggregation is agreed.

H3Economic stress and housing insecurity raise lethalityNeeds data

If true

Shelter denials and length of stay track homicide counts. New York denied shelter to 24,635 people in 2024 and the average city stay was 115 days, nearly three times the rest of the state.

How we test it

Year-by-year state dashboard figures against homicide counts by county, controlling for population.

H4The pandemic reset the baseline and it has not come backPartly supported

If true

A step change in 2020 to 2022 that persists. New York City's homicide series fits this shape; incident reports do not show the same step.

How we test it

Interrupted time series on monthly city data from 2017 to 2025.

H5Deaths in the sex trade are the missing denominatorCore claim

If true

A material share of women killed by men in New York are killed by buyers or exploiters, and these cases are filed outside domestic violence or not identified as femicide at all.

How we test it

Count sex-trade cases in Women Count USA by state and year, then read how each was reported. This is what the project exists to produce.

City counts: ENDGBV annual reports 2022 to 2024. Backlash figure: UN Women, Women's Rights in Review 30 Years After Beijing (March 2025).

Thirty years of saying it at the United Nations

The world has agreed, in writing, to end violence against women many times. The question for 2026 is whether the language is getting stronger or being traded away. Purple marks the moments of pushback.

1979

CEDAW adopted

The Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women. The United States signed in 1980 and has never ratified it.

1993

Declaration on the Elimination of Violence against Women

The General Assembly defines violence against women for the first time, including violence in the family, in the community and condoned by the state.

1995

Beijing Declaration and Platform for Action

189 governments adopt twelve critical areas of concern. Violence against women is one. Trafficking and prostitution are named inside it.

2000

Security Council resolution 1325

Women, peace and security enters the Security Council's agenda. Nine follow-on resolutions by 2019.

2013

General Assembly resolution 68/191 on gender-related killing of women and girls

The first UN resolution on femicide by name. Adopted by the General Assembly, not the Security Council, and renewed as 70/176 in 2015. It asks states to collect data and to criminalise and investigate these killings.

2013

CSW57 agreed conclusions

The Commission on the Status of Women agrees conclusions on eliminating violence against women after a failure to agree in 2003 and 2012.

2016 to 2017

Security Council resolutions 2331 and 2388

Trafficking in persons in conflict situations. The closest the Council has come to CATW's subject, and the only Council hook for it.

2015

Sustainable Development Goal 5

Target 5.2 commits every state to eliminate all forms of violence against women and girls, including trafficking and sexual exploitation, by 2030.

2024

One in four governments report a backlash

In UN Women's Beijing+30 review, nearly a quarter of governments say women's rights faced a backlash in 2024.

March 2025

Beijing+30 political declaration

CSW69 adopts a political declaration on the thirtieth anniversary after difficult negotiations. Civil society groups say the consensus came at the cost of language on sexual and reproductive rights.

November 2025

Femicides in 2024

UNODC and UN Women publish the first global femicide estimate built from 117 countries. One woman or girl every ten minutes. Fewer countries are reporting than before.

2026

New York

No femicide definition in state law. This project, with CATW, builds the case for one.

Why the Security Council does not address femicide

The Council's mandate is international peace and security. Its gender agenda, Women, Peace and Security, covers women in armed conflict and conflict-related sexual violence, not killings in peacetime. Femicide is handled as crime and human rights by the General Assembly's Third Committee, the Commission on the Status of Women, UNODC, UN Women, the CEDAW Committee and the Special Rapporteur on violence against women. The Council is also the one body where a single veto blocks a new agenda item, and several permanent members have resisted widening its gender work. The practical venues for New York's case are therefore the General Assembly femicide resolutions and CSW, with the Council's trafficking resolutions as the one point of contact.

Does it change the outcome?

The strongest evidence that women in office change what government does comes from a natural experiment, not a correlation.

265 villages
India randomly reserves one third of village council leaderships for women. Where a woman led, councils invested more in the public goods women asked for, such as drinking water. Chattopadhyay and Duflo, Econometrica, 2004. Randomised, so the "richer places elect women" objection does not apply.
Open
Whether women heads of government or CEOs produce better profits, health or quality of life at national scale is contested. Observational studies cannot separate the effect of the leader from the effect of being the kind of country or firm that chooses her. The backlog lists the studies to review with their confidence intervals.
Not here
This site shows no causal claim about leadership. It shows who is in the room, and lets the reader hold that against what the room has decided.

IPU, Women's representation in parliament sees sluggish gains, March 2026. Chattopadhyay and Duflo, Women as Policy Makers, Econometrica 72(5), 2004.

Advance, stall, regress: the law on paper

The World Bank has scored every economy's laws on women's economic rights since 1970. The score rose for fifty years, slowed sharply after 2021, and fell by a third when the Bank started measuring whether the laws are actually implemented.
